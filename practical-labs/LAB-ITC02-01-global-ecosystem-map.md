# LAB-ITC02-01 — Build a Global Trade Ecosystem Map

**Level:** Intermediate | **Time:** 90–120 minutes

## Scenario
A Thai importer buys industrial components from a manufacturer in South Korea through a trading company. The goods ship by sea. The importer appoints a forwarder, customs broker and cargo insurer. Payment is in USD through a commercial bank.

## Deliverables
1. **Party register:** At least 12 parties/organisations, their country, role, contract link, authority, contact owner and evidence.
2. **Four-flow map:** Goods, information, money and regulatory evidence.
3. **Role distinctions:** Identify buyer, seller, manufacturer, brand owner (if any), exporter, importer, consignee, forwarder, carrier, broker, bank and insurer.
4. **RACI:** At least 10 activities and one accountable owner per activity.
5. **Risk points:** Mark at least five hand-offs where errors, delay, fraud or damage could occur.

## Model review
- The trading company may be seller if it contracts to resell the goods; verify the actual contract.
- The manufacturer is the factory producing the goods; it may not issue the sales invoice.
- The forwarder is not automatically the carrier.
- The bank does not verify physical product quality.
- Customs authorities make official decisions under local law; a broker supports declarations within authorisation.
- Country of origin must be determined under applicable origin rules, not assumed from ship-from location.

## Quality checklist
- [ ] Every arrow has a source and destination.
- [ ] Goods and money flows are not conflated.
- [ ] Contractual roles are labelled.
- [ ] Unknown information is marked “to verify”.
- [ ] Authority and escalation boundaries are clear.
- [ ] Evidence source and date are recorded.

# ITC-03-LES-05 — Purchase Orders and Order Acknowledgement

**Level:** Intermediate | **Study time:** 90 minutes

## 1. Learning outcomes
Create a controlled PO, understand its relationship with quotation and contract, reconcile supplier acknowledgement and manage changes.

## 2. Purchase order definition
A **purchase order (PO)** is a buyer-issued commercial document specifying the goods/services ordered and agreed purchase details. Depending on governing law and prior communications, it may form or evidence a contract; do not assume the same legal effect in every jurisdiction.

## 3. Essential PO fields
Unique PO number/date; buyer/seller legal names; ship-to/bill-to; product code and exact description; specification revision; quantity/unit; unit and total price; currency; tax treatment; delivery term/named place/edition; required date; packaging; inspection; warranty; payment terms; required documents; authorised signatory; applicable contract reference; change-control instructions.

## 4. Order acknowledgement
Supplier acknowledgement should confirm:
- PO number and revision.
- Accepted product/model/specification.
- Quantity and unit.
- Price and currency.
- Delivery term and named place/version.
- Cargo-ready/delivery date.
- Payment and warranty terms.
- Deviations and exclusions.
- Supplier authorised confirmation.

A supplier's acknowledgement containing different terms may be a counter-offer or contractual issue. Escalate; do not treat it as automatic acceptance.

## 5. Three-way commercial control
Where relevant, match:
1. Approved purchase order.
2. Supplier invoice.
3. Goods receipt / service acceptance.

Differences in price, quantity, model or bank details must be investigated under the organisation's controls before payment.

## 6. Change order
Record change ID, original PO, requested change, reason, cost/time/compliance impact, buyer approver, supplier acknowledgement, revised PO version and affected shipment documents. Preserve prior version.

## 7. Common errors
Duplicate PO; wrong legal entity; missing currency; inconsistent delivery term; obsolete model; unclear tax; unsigned variation; paying against an unapproved invoice.

## 8. Practice
Prepare a PO for a fictional order and compare a supplier acknowledgement that changes quantity and delivery date. Produce a discrepancy log and approval request.

## 9. Knowledge check
1. Is every supplier acknowledgement unconditional acceptance? **No.**
2. Why use PO revisions? **To control changes and identify the operative order.**
3. What does three-way matching compare? **PO, invoice and receipt/acceptance evidence.**

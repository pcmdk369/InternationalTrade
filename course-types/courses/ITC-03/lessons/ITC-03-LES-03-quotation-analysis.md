# ITC-03-LES-03 — Quotation Analysis and Comparison

**Level:** Intermediate | **Study time:** 90 minutes

## 1. Learning outcomes
Read supplier quotations, normalise commercial assumptions, calculate comparable cost and document a defensible recommendation.

## 2. Quotation fields
Supplier identity; quote number/date; validity; item/model; specification revision; quantity/unit; unit price; total; currency; taxes; packaging; tooling; freight; insurance; delivery term/named place/version; lead time; payment terms; warranty; exclusions; minimum order; offer conditions.

## 3. Like-for-like comparison
Never compare only the headline unit price. Check:
- Same product specification and quality.
- Same quantity and unit of measure.
- Same currency and quote validity.
- Same delivery basis and named place.
- Included/excluded freight, insurance, duties and local charges.
- Same payment timing and credit cost.
- Lead time measured from the same trigger.
- Warranty, inspection and after-sales scope.

## 4. Example calculation
Supplier A: 100 units × USD 42 = USD 4,200; origin handling USD 180; freight USD 620; insurance USD 40; estimated destination charges USD 300. Illustrative total before any unlisted duties/taxes = USD 5,340, or USD 53.40 per unit.

Supplier B: 100 units × USD 47 = USD 4,700; origin handling included; freight USD 350; insurance USD 40; destination charges USD 250. Illustrative total = USD 5,340, or USD 53.40 per unit.

The lower unit-price offer is not cheaper on these assumptions. Verify that cost items are not duplicated and that the delivery terms make the costs buyer-payable.

## 5. Weighted scorecard
Use mandatory pass/fail gates for legal identity, product compliance, safety and required capability. Then score cost, quality, lead time, capacity, service and risk (e.g. 1–5) using approved weights totalling 100%. Record evidence and rationale. Do not allow a weighted score to override a failed mandatory gate.

## 6. Recommendation memo
Include purpose, scope, suppliers invited, bids received, deviations, normalised cost, risk/quality score, assumptions, missing information, recommendation, approval required and validity deadline.

## 7. Practice
Build a comparison for three offers with different currencies, freight inclusions and lead times. State the exchange-rate source/date and assumptions. Escalate uncertain duty, tax or tariff questions to qualified specialists.

## 8. Knowledge check
1. What makes quotations comparable? **Common specification, quantity, currency basis, delivery scope and assumptions.**
2. Should estimated duties be presented as confirmed? **No; label assumptions and verify official rates/classification.**
3. What is a mandatory gate? **A condition that must pass before commercial scoring.**

# ITC-03-LES-08 — Import & Export Operations Assessment

**Level:** Intermediate | **Study time:** 2–3 hours

## 1. Assessment weights
- RFQ/specification pack: 20%
- Quotation analysis: 20%
- PO and order-control file: 25%
- Integrated case: 35%

Suggested pass: 70%. Critical document-integrity, product-safety and compliance failures require remediation.

## 2. Knowledge quiz
1. Name six required fields in an RFQ.
2. Why must suppliers quote against the same specification revision?
3. What is landed-cost comparison intended to prevent?
4. What should a coordinator do when a supplier acknowledgement changes the PO quantity?
5. What is a deviation schedule?
6. Does cargo-ready mean export-cleared?
7. What does ETA mean?
8. What evidence is useful at delivery?
9. What is three-way matching?
10. Why preserve superseded PO versions?

### Answer key
1. Any six: product/spec, quantity/unit, quality, packaging, destination/date, currency, delivery term, payment, required documents, validity and response deadline.
2. To ensure offers are comparable and the supplier prices the approved requirement.
3. Misleading decisions based only on unit price or incomplete charges.
4. Place the change on hold, assess impact and obtain authorised approval/revised PO and acknowledgement.
5. List of supplier exceptions against the requested specification/terms.
6. No.
7. Estimated time of arrival.
8. POD/receipt, quantity/condition record, exception notation and photos where appropriate.
9. PO, supplier invoice and goods receipt/acceptance.
10. To preserve traceability and prove which revision was approved.

## 3. Integrated case
A Thai importer requests 500 electric motors from a supplier abroad. Three quotations arrive in different currencies and with different delivery terms. The preferred supplier acknowledges the PO but changes the model suffix and delivery date. Production later reports a two-week delay. The final invoice includes an unapproved tooling charge.

### Tasks
A. Create an RFQ data checklist and identify missing technical information.  
B. Normalise the quotations and list assumptions.  
C. Write a negotiation plan with authority limits.  
D. Prepare a PO discrepancy log for model and date changes.  
E. Create a production and cargo-readiness tracker.  
F. Prepare a shipment exception report for the delay.  
G. Reconcile invoice against PO and receipt; decide what must be held/escalated.  
H. Produce a final order closure checklist.

## 4. Model response
Do not approve the model change without technical approval and compliance review. Do not silently accept the revised date; update the delivery plan and assess impact. Challenge the tooling charge against the approved quotation/PO and obtain authorised change evidence before payment. Record each decision and retain the original documents.

## 5. Practical portfolio
Submit RFQ, clarification log, supplier comparison, approval memo, PO, acknowledgement comparison, production tracker, shipment exception report, delivery receipt and closure reconciliation.

## 6. Marking rubric
| Criterion | Full-credit standard |
|---|---|
| Specification | Complete, version-controlled and unambiguous |
| Cost analysis | Like-for-like, assumptions explicit |
| Approval | Decisions within authority and evidenced |
| Order control | Discrepancies and changes traceable |
| Execution | Milestones, owners and exceptions maintained |
| Closure | Delivery and invoice reconciled |

## 7. Professional reminder
This module teaches operational coordination, not legal advice. Confirm contract interpretation, import/export obligations, tax, permits and product controls with qualified specialists and current official sources.

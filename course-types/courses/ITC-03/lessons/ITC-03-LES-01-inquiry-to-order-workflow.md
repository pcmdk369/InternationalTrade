# ITC-03-LES-01 — Inquiry-to-Order Workflow

**Level:** Intermediate | **Study time:** 75–90 minutes

## Learning outcomes
Convert a vague customer request into a controlled inquiry; identify missing information; define stage gates from inquiry to confirmed order.

## 1. Definition
The **inquiry-to-order process** is the controlled workflow that captures a buyer's requirement, validates it, obtains and evaluates supplier offers, secures approvals and creates an accepted order.

**हिंदी:** Inquiry-to-order प्रक्रिया ग्राहक की आवश्यकता समझने, जानकारी सत्यापित करने, quotation लेने, तुलना करने, approval लेने और order confirm करने की प्रक्रिया है।  
**ไทย:** กระบวนการ Inquiry-to-order คือการรับความต้องการ ตรวจสอบข้อมูล ขอใบเสนอราคา เปรียบเทียบ อนุมัติ และยืนยันคำสั่งซื้อ

## 2. Workflow
1. Receive inquiry and acknowledge receipt.
2. Identify buyer, destination and intended use.
3. Capture product specification, quantity, unit, packaging and quality criteria.
4. Confirm required delivery date and delivery location.
5. Identify compliance, permit and technical questions early.
6. Prepare RFQ and invite qualified suppliers.
7. Receive and clarify quotations.
8. Compare total cost, lead time, quality, capacity, terms and risk.
9. Negotiate within delegated authority.
10. Obtain internal commercial, technical, finance and compliance approvals.
11. Issue purchase order or sales confirmation.
12. Obtain written supplier acknowledgement and reconcile differences.

## 3. Minimum inquiry data
- Request ID and date.
- Buyer legal name and contact.
- Product name, model, technical specification and revision.
- Quantity and unit of measure.
- Packaging and labelling requirements.
- Delivery location and required date.
- Proposed currency and payment terms.
- Proposed Incoterms® rule, named place and edition if relevant.
- Inspection, testing and warranty requirements.
- Destination product standards/permit questions.
- Attachments, drawings, photos and confidentiality level.

Do not accept ambiguous units such as “10 boxes” without defining units per box and total quantity.

## 4. Stage gates
- Gate A: inquiry complete enough to quote.
- Gate B: technical specification approved.
- Gate C: supplier and compliance screening complete.
- Gate D: quotation comparison and budget approved.
- Gate E: contract/PO issued by authorised person.
- Gate F: supplier acknowledgement matches approved order.

## 5. Example
Inquiry: “Need 500 pumps urgently.” This is insufficient. Ask for model, capacity, voltage, material, intended use, certification, quantity unit, destination, target date, packaging, warranty and required documentation. Record unanswered questions; do not invent technical details.

## 6. Common errors
Wrong unit; outdated specification; unclear destination; comparing different delivery terms; assuming a quotation is a binding accepted order; bypassing approval; failing to record changes.

## 7. Practical assignment
Take a hypothetical request for 100 industrial fans. Create a clarification log with at least 12 questions, then turn the verified answers into an RFQ cover sheet.

## 8. Knowledge check
1. Why define unit of measure? **To avoid quantity and pricing ambiguity.**
2. Should a coordinator invent missing specifications? **No; obtain written clarification.**
3. What proves supplier acceptance of the PO? **Written acknowledgement/contract evidence consistent with the agreed process.**

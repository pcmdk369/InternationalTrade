# ITC-01-LES-08 — Module Review and Assessment

**Level:** Intermediate foundation | **Study time:** 2–3 hours  
**Languages:** English, Hindi and Thai

## 1. Purpose
This lesson consolidates ITC-01 and assesses whether the learner can explain core trade concepts, map a transaction and identify issues that require specialist escalation.

## 2. Essential concept review
- International trade concerns cross-border/customs-territory exchange.
- Domestic and international transactions differ in jurisdiction, customs, currency, documentation and logistics complexity.
- Import, export, re-export and transit describe different customs/movement contexts.
- Commercial parties and logistics parties have distinct roles; a forwarder is not automatically the carrier.
- Trade can create value through specialisation, comparative advantage, scale, technology and market access.
- A trade transaction moves through inquiry, qualification, negotiation, order, readiness, export, carriage, import, delivery, payment and closure.
- Risks require owners, controls, evidence and escalation.

## 4. Knowledge quiz
Choose or write the best answer.

1. International trade is best described as:  
   A. Any sale over 100 km; B. Exchange across borders/customs territories; C. Online sales only.
2. A Thai company purchases goods from Japan for use in Thailand. The Thai movement is generally:  
   A. Import; B. Transit; C. Domestic sale.
3. Cargo changes from one ship to another at a hub. This event is:  
   A. Re-export in every case; B. Transshipment; C. Domestic trade.
4. Comparative advantage is based on:  
   A. Opportunity cost; B. Company size; C. Invoice value.
5. A freight forwarder is always the carrier. True/False.
6. A notify party is automatically the owner of the goods. True/False.
7. Name four milestones in a normal trade lifecycle.
8. Name three risks that are more prominent in cross-border transactions.
9. What should a coordinator do when a product's permit requirement is unclear?
10. What is the safe procedure for a supplier bank-detail change?

### Answer key
1. B. 2. A. 3. B. 4. A. 5. False. 6. False.  
7. Any four: order confirmation, cargo readiness, booking, departure, arrival, customs clearance, delivery, payment, closure.  
8. Any three: FX, customs/regulatory, sanctions, international logistics, country risk, cross-border fraud.  
9. Hold the relevant commitment/shipment and obtain confirmation from the competent authority or qualified specialist/broker. 10. Hold change/payment, verify using a previously trusted channel, obtain dual approval and retain evidence.

## 5. Integrated case
A Thai importer buys 300 electric water pumps from a supplier in another country. The supplier's quotation is in USD. The buyer has a target delivery date and asks the coordinator to arrange the shipment. The commercial invoice later shows a different model number from the approved purchase order. The supplier also emails new bank details.

### Tasks
A. Identify buyer, seller, manufacturer, forwarder, carrier, broker, consignee and bank roles.  
B. List information required before accepting the shipment.  
C. Map the lifecycle and stage gates.  
D. Identify at least six risks and controls.  
E. Draft an exception log for the model discrepancy and bank-detail change.  
F. List records to retain in the shipment file.

### Model answer outline
- Pause document approval and payment; do not silently amend the invoice or PO.
- Verify exact model/specification with buyer and seller through authorised channels; obtain a corrected document or approved order change.
- Independently verify bank details using a known contact and dual approval.
- Confirm product regulatory requirements, technical data, classification research, origin, packaging, route, permits, delivery term, insurance and payment conditions.
- Coordinate with authorised customs broker and logistics provider; retain declarations, permits, invoices, packing list, transport record, inspection and delivery evidence.
- Escalate legal classification, controlled-goods, sanctions and product-safety questions to competent specialists.

## 9. Important limitation
This module teaches operational concepts, not legal advice. Customs, sanctions, tax, licensing, product standards, origin and documentary requirements change by jurisdiction and product. Verify current official rules before any real shipment.

# ITC-01-LES-07 — Trade Risks and Operational Controls

**Level:** Intermediate | **Study time:** 90 minutes  
**Languages:** English, Hindi and Thai

## 1. Learning outcomes
Recognise common cross-border trade risks, rate them consistently, define preventive/detective controls and escalate issues.

## 2. Risk definition
A **risk** is the effect of uncertainty on objectives. A risk statement should identify a cause, an uncertain event and its consequence.

Example: Because the supplier's production capacity has not been verified (cause), the order may miss the cargo-ready date (event), causing a production stoppage and extra cost (consequence).

**हिंदी:** जोखिम अनिश्चितता के कारण लक्ष्य पर पड़ने वाला प्रभाव है।  
**ไทย:** ความเสี่ยงคือผลกระทบจากความไม่แน่นอนต่อเป้าหมาย

## 3. Risk categories
- Commercial: price, quality, quantity, contract ambiguity.
- Counterparty: fraud, insolvency, false identity.
- Logistics: delay, loss, damage, capacity, route disruption.
- Customs/regulatory: incorrect declaration, missing permit, restricted goods.
- Financial: FX movement, non-payment, bank/document discrepancy.
- Legal/sanctions: prohibited parties, controlled goods, changing restrictions.
- Operational: data errors, missed milestones, weak handovers.
- Cyber/fraud: invoice interception, spoofed email, payment diversion.
- ESG/reputation: labour, environmental or sourcing concerns.

## 4. Risk scoring
Use a documented 1–5 scale for likelihood and impact. A simple inherent risk score is likelihood × impact. Define bands in the organisation's policy; for training, 1–4 low, 5–9 moderate, 10–16 high and 17–25 critical may be used as an illustrative scale only. A score never overrides a mandatory legal prohibition or safety stop.

## 5. Controls
- **Preventive:** supplier verification, dual approval, approved specifications, access controls, permits before dispatch.
- **Detective:** reconciliation, inspection, milestone alerts, bank callback, document review.
- **Corrective:** hold cargo/payment, amend documents through authorised process, notify insurer/broker, activate alternate supplier.
- **Directive:** written policies, training and role separation.

| R01 | Supplier identity fraud | 2 | 5 | Independent registry/bank verification | Procurement | New supplier or bank change |

| R05 | Cargo damage | 2 | 4 | Packaging standard and insurance review | Logistics | Loading/damage event |

Scores are examples, not measured probabilities.

## 7. High-risk fraud control: payment changes
If a supplier emails new bank details:
1. Stop the change and flag the invoice/payment.
2. Call a previously verified phone number, not the number in the change email.
3. Require dual approval and independent verification.
4. Record who verified, when and what evidence was used.
5. Notify finance/security if compromise is suspected.

## 8. Incident response
Contain → preserve evidence → assess safety/legal/financial impact → notify authorised owners → engage insurer/broker/lawyer/authority as appropriate → agree corrective action → document closure → update risk register.

Do not destroy original emails, alter records or make unsupported admissions of liability.

## 9. Practice
For each risk, state cause, event, consequence, likelihood, impact, preventive control, detective control and owner:
- Supplier cannot meet capacity.
- HS code or product description is uncertain.
- Cargo is wet on arrival.
- Buyer receives a fraudulent bank-account change request.

## 10. Knowledge check
1. Can a low risk score permit prohibited trade? **No.**
2. What is the first action for suspicious payment changes? **Place the change/payment on hold and independently verify.**
3. Name three control types. **Preventive, detective, corrective, directive.**
4. What is residual risk? **Risk remaining after controls.**

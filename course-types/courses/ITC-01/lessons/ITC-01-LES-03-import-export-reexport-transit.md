# ITC-01-LES-03 — Imports, Exports, Re-exports and Transit

**Level:** Foundation to intermediate | **Study time:** 60–90 minutes  
**Languages:** English, Hindi and Thai

## 1. Learning outcomes
Define import, export, re-export and transit; distinguish physical movement from ownership and commercial invoicing; classify basic scenarios with appropriate caution.

## 2. Definitions
- **Import:** Bringing goods into a country's customs territory under the applicable import procedure.
- **Export:** Sending goods out of a country's customs territory under the applicable export procedure.
- **Re-export:** Exporting goods that were previously imported. Customs regimes may treat returned, stored, processed or temporarily admitted goods differently.
- **Transit:** Moving goods through a territory under a customs transit procedure, generally with customs control and prescribed guarantees or documentation where required.
- **Transshipment:** Transferring cargo from one vessel/aircraft/vehicle to another during a journey. It is a logistics event and is not automatically the same as re-export.
- **Third-country trade / merchanting:** A commercial arrangement where a trader buys from one country and sells to another while goods may move directly between those countries. Exact statistical and customs treatment varies.

**हिंदी:** Import = आयात; Export = निर्यात; Re-export = पुनः निर्यात; Transit = पारगमन।  
**ไทย:** Import = การนำเข้า; Export = การส่งออก; Re-export = การส่งออกซ้ำ; Transit = การผ่านแดน

## 3. Three questions to classify a transaction
1. Where are the goods physically located now?
2. Which customs territory do they enter, leave or pass through?
3. What customs procedure is declared and accepted by the relevant authority?

A sales invoice alone does not establish the customs procedure.

## 4. Worked scenarios
**Scenario A:** A Thai buyer purchases coffee machines from Italy and imports them into Thailand. The movement is an export from the relevant Italian/EU customs context and an import into Thailand, subject to the applicable procedures.

**Scenario B:** A Thai distributor imports 500 lamps, stores them under a customs regime, then sends 100 to another country. This may be a re-export, but the exact treatment depends on the original procedure, processing and customs records.

**Scenario C:** A container travels from one country through a neighbouring country to its destination under a transit procedure. The intermediate country is a transit territory; it is not automatically the final importing country.

**Scenario D:** A container is unloaded at a hub and loaded onto another ship. This is transshipment. Whether customs transit or another procedure also applies depends on the route and local rules.

## 5. Classification worksheet

| Situation | Likely description | What to verify |
|---|---|---|
| Goods enter the buyer's country from abroad | Import | Import declaration and product requirements |
| Goods leave seller's country for a foreign buyer | Export | Export declaration and restrictions |
| Previously imported goods are sent abroad | Re-export may apply | Original customs regime and condition of goods |
| Goods cross a territory en route | Transit may apply | Transit procedure, seals, time limits and guarantee |
| Cargo changes vessel at a hub | Transshipment | Port handling and customs status |

## 6. Coordinator workflow
1. Obtain the commercial instruction and purchase/sales contract.
2. Obtain full product and packaging details.
3. Map ship-from, loading, transit, discharge and final-delivery locations.
4. Ask the customs broker which procedure applies in each territory.
5. Confirm permits, declarations, deadlines, guarantees and documentary requirements.
6. Record the procedure and responsible party in the shipment file.
7. Reconcile actual movement with customs and transport evidence.

## 7. Common errors
- Calling every cross-border movement a re-export.
- Confusing transshipment with customs transit.
- Assuming the invoice seller is the physical exporter.
- Assuming goods can enter a country because the carrier accepts them.
- Ignoring temporary admission, bonded storage or inward/outward processing rules.

## 8. Practice and answer key
1. Goods are shipped from Thailand to a buyer in Laos. **Export from Thailand; import into Laos**, subject to the applicable customs procedures.
2. A shipment passes through a third country under a formal transit procedure. **Transit** in the intermediate territory.
3. Goods move from one ship to another at a port. **Transshipment** as a logistics event; check the customs procedure separately.
4. Previously imported goods are sent back to the supplier. **Potential re-export/return procedure**; verify local rules and original import status.

## 9. Knowledge check
- Does a commercial resale automatically prove re-export? **No.**
- Is transshipment always transit? **No.**
- Who confirms the legal customs procedure? **The competent customs authority, normally supported by a qualified broker or customs specialist.**

# ITC-01-LES-05 — Trade Participants and Their Roles

**Level:** Foundation to intermediate | **Study time:** 75–90 minutes  
**Languages:** English, Hindi and Thai

## 1. Learning outcomes
Identify the main parties in a trade transaction, distinguish contractual roles from operational roles, and prepare a responsibility/escalation map.

## 2. Participants
- **Buyer / importer:** Purchases goods and arranges obligations allocated to it by contract and applicable law.
- **Seller / exporter:** Sells and supplies goods; may have export and delivery obligations.
- **Manufacturer:** Produces goods; may differ from seller/exporter.
- **Supplier / sourcing agent:** Provides goods or connects buyer and producer.
- **Consignee:** Party named to receive cargo in the transport document; legal meaning depends on document and context.
- **Notify party:** Contact to be notified about shipment arrival; not automatically the owner or payer.
- **Freight forwarder:** Arranges transport and related logistics as agent or principal depending on contract and service.
- **Carrier:** Undertakes carriage by sea, air, road, rail or multimodal means.
- **Customs broker / declarant:** Supports or submits customs declarations under local authorisation and law.
- **Insurer:** Provides cover according to policy terms, exclusions and conditions.
- **Bank / payment provider:** Handles payment, collections, documentary credits or trade finance.
- **Inspection / testing body:** Performs agreed inspections or tests within its scope.
- **Port, terminal, warehouse and handling operator:** Provides cargo handling and storage services.
- **Government authorities:** Customs, standards, food/drug, agriculture, licensing and other competent agencies.

## 3. Important distinctions
- A freight forwarder is not necessarily the carrier.
- A consignee is not automatically the importer of record.
- A notify party is not automatically liable for payment.
- A manufacturer may not be the exporter shown on documents.
- A customs broker does not replace the importer's responsibility to provide accurate information.
- A bank checks documents under the applicable banking rules and undertaking; it does not normally inspect the physical goods.

## 4. Responsibility matrix (illustrative only)
| Activity | Primary owner often assigned | Coordinator's check |
|---|---|---|
| Product specification | Buyer + seller | Approved revision and tolerance |
| Export packing | Seller/packer | Marks, dimensions, protection |
| Export declaration | Contractually assigned exporter/broker | Filing evidence and accuracy |
| Main carriage | Party named by contract/Incoterms rule | Booking and transport document |
| Import declaration | Importer/authorised broker | Permit and declaration status |
| Cargo insurance | Party responsible under contract/rule | Policy, insured value and exclusions |
| Payment | Buyer and seller through agreed channel | Due date and bank evidence |

This table is not a legal allocation. Confirm each contract and the chosen Incoterms® rule, named place and version.

## 5. RACI method
- **R — Responsible:** Performs the task.
- **A — Accountable:** Owns the outcome; normally one A per activity.
- **C — Consulted:** Provides input.
- **I — Informed:** Receives updates.

### Mini example: shipment booking
Buyer: C; Seller: A/R (if seller controls booking); Forwarder: R; Carrier: R for carriage acceptance; Coordinator: R for chasing milestones; Manager: I. The actual RACI must match the agreement.

## 6. Escalation rules
Escalate immediately when:
- Product description or quantity differs between documents.
- Permit or licence is missing or expired.
- Sanctions, restricted-party or controlled-goods concern arises.
- Payment instructions change unexpectedly.
- Cargo is damaged, short-shipped or delayed beyond tolerance.
- A party requests an inaccurate declaration or altered document.
- A safety incident or suspected fraud occurs.

Never alter a commercial or customs document to conceal a discrepancy. Preserve the original record and obtain authorised correction.

## 7. Practical assignment
Choose a hypothetical shipment and create:
1. Stakeholder list with legal names and contacts.
2. Contractual role for each party.
3. RACI for 10 activities.
4. Escalation contact tree.
5. Evidence required to close each task.

## 8. Knowledge check
1. Is the forwarder always the carrier? **No.**
2. Does notify party automatically own the goods? **No.**
3. What does RACI stand for? **Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed.**
4. Who should approve a change to payment bank details? **An authorised buyer/seller representative using independently verified controls; never rely only on an email request.**

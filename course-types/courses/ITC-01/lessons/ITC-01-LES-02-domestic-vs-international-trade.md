# ITC-01-LES-02 — Domestic Trade vs International Trade

**Level:** Foundation to intermediate | **Study time:** 60–90 minutes  
**Languages:** English, Hindi and Thai

## 1. Learning outcomes
After this lesson, the learner can compare domestic and international trade, identify cross-border complexity, and select the right operational checks before accepting an order.

## 2. Core definitions
**Domestic trade** is the buying and selling of goods or services within one country's customs territory, under that jurisdiction's applicable rules.

**International trade** is the exchange of goods or services across national borders or customs territories. It may involve export, import, transit, foreign exchange, international transport and multiple legal systems.

**हिंदी:** घरेलू व्यापार एक ही देश के भीतर होता है। अंतरराष्ट्रीय व्यापार में देश या customs territories के बीच वस्तुओं या सेवाओं/सेवाओं का लेन-देन होता है।  
**ไทย:** การค้าภายในประเทศคือการซื้อขายภายในประเทศเดียว ส่วนการค้าระหว่างประเทศเกี่ยวข้องกับการซื้อขายข้ามพรมแดนหรือเขตศุลกากร

## 3. Comparison

| Dimension | Domestic trade | International trade |
|---|---|---|
| Parties | Usually within one jurisdiction | Parties may be in different jurisdictions |
| Currency | Often one local currency | May involve multiple currencies |
| Regulation | Domestic laws and tax rules | Export/import controls, customs and destination rules |
| Transport | Domestic networks | May include ports, airports, borders and transshipment |
| Documents | Domestic invoice/order records | May require commercial invoice, packing list, transport and origin documents |
| Risk | Delivery, quality, credit | Also FX, customs, sanctions, country and cross-border risks |
| Time | Often shorter | Depends on border, mode, route and clearance |

Not every international transaction requires every document listed above. Requirements depend on the product, route, customs regime, contract and current law.

## 4. Why the distinction matters
A coordinator must not treat an overseas purchase as an ordinary domestic purchase. Before confirming an order, identify:
1. Seller's legal country and ship-from location.
2. Buyer's legal country and final destination.
3. Product description, composition, intended use and quantity.
4. Whether the goods are controlled, restricted, dangerous or subject to permits.
5. Proposed route and transport mode.
6. Currency, payment method and agreed delivery rule.
7. Who is responsible for export clearance, import clearance, duties and delivery.

## 5. Worked example
A retailer in Bangkok buys 100 office chairs from a supplier in Chiang Mai. This is domestic trade within Thailand, even if the supplier uses a long-distance truck.

The same retailer buys 100 chairs from a manufacturer in Vietnam and imports them into Thailand. This is international trade. The coordinator must verify product requirements, export and import procedures, transport documents, applicable taxes/duties and delivery responsibilities.

**Key point:** Distance alone does not make trade international. The relevant border/customs-territory and legal context matters.

## 6. Operational checklist
- [ ] Confirm buyer, seller and consignee legal identities.
- [ ] Confirm country of origin separately from ship-from country.
- [ ] Confirm destination and any transit countries.
- [ ] Confirm product classification research is assigned to a competent person.
- [ ] Confirm permits and restrictions before shipment.
- [ ] Record currency and payment terms.
- [ ] Record delivery term, named place and version where applicable.
- [ ] Save evidence and approval trail.

## 7. Practice
Classify:
A. A shop in Phuket buys stock from a wholesaler in Bangkok.  
B. A Thai importer buys machinery from Japan for delivery to Laem Chabang.  
C. A Thai company sells goods to a buyer in Malaysia.  
D. A company buys goods abroad and sells them abroad without bringing them into its own domestic market.

**Answer key:** A domestic; B import/international; C export/international; D potentially merchanting or a third-country/re-export structure depending on ownership, movement and customs treatment. Do not classify solely from the invoice address.

## 8. Knowledge check
1. Is a domestic truck journey international trade because it is 700 km? **No.**
2. Name four additional checks often needed in cross-border trade. **Customs, product controls/permits, currency/payment, transport/documentation** (other valid answers include origin, sanctions and tax).
3. Is country of origin always the same as ship-from country? **No.**

## 9. Professional standard
Never promise a delivery date, duty rate or legal clearance outcome before checking the route, product, current rules and responsible authority/broker. Escalate uncertain legal classification to a qualified customs professional.

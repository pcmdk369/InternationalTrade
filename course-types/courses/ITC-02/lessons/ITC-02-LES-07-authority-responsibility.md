# ITC-02-LES-07 — Authority, Responsibility and Governance

**Level:** Intermediate to advanced | **Study time:** 90 minutes | **Languages:** English, Hindi, Thai

## 1. Learning outcomes
Distinguish accountability, responsibility, authority and liability; build a RACI and delegated authority matrix; establish approval, escalation and evidence controls.

## 2. Four concepts
- **Responsibility:** Duty to perform a task or produce an output.
- **Accountability:** Ownership of the result and answerability for it.
- **Authority:** Permission or delegated power to make a decision or bind an organisation.
- **Liability:** Legal or contractual exposure determined by law and agreement.

These concepts are related but not interchangeable. A coordinator may be responsible for collecting a customs document but have no authority to classify goods or sign a declaration.

**हिंदी:** Responsibility काम करने की जिम्मेदारी है; Accountability परिणाम की जवाबदेही है; Authority निर्णय लेने का अधिकार है; Liability कानूनी/अनुबंधीय दायित्व है।  
**ไทย:** Responsibility คือหน้าที่ปฏิบัติงาน Accountability คือความรับผิดชอบต่อผลลัพธ์ Authority คืออำนาจอนุมัติ และ Liability คือความรับผิดตามกฎหมายหรือสัญญา

## 3. RACI design rules
- Assign one accountable owner for each activity.
- Assign at least one responsible performer.
- Consult people before decisions where their expertise is needed.
- Keep informed recipients proportionate.
- Avoid making every stakeholder responsible for everything.
- Match the matrix to contract, policy and legal authorisation.

## 4. Delegated authority matrix
Example only; each company must set thresholds and roles.

| Decision | Coordinator | Trade manager | Finance | Compliance/legal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Update shipment ETA tracker | Approve within process | Informed | — | — |
| Change product specification | Collect request only | Approve per delegation | — | Consult if regulated |
| Accept supplier price change | No authority unless delegated | Approve within limit | Review budget | Consult if contract issue |
| Change bank details | Cannot approve alone | Informed | Dual verification/approval | Escalate suspected fraud |
| Confirm HS classification | Gather product data | Assign qualified reviewer | — | Specialist/broker review |
| Release shipment with missing permit | No | No unilateral override | — | Competent authority/legal confirmation |

## 5. Segregation of duties
Where practical, separate:
- Supplier creation from payment approval.
- Bank-detail change entry from independent verification.
- Purchase order creation from final approval.
- Goods receipt from invoice payment release.
- Customs data preparation from authorised declaration approval.

Small organisations may not have enough staff for full separation. Use compensating controls such as owner review, dual approval and independent reconciliation.

## 6. Decision and change log
Record request ID, date/time, requester, description, reason, impact, options, approver, authority reference, decision, implementation owner, evidence and closure date. Preserve previous versions.

## 7. Escalation thresholds
Escalate immediately for suspected fraud, sanctions or restricted-party match, unsafe product, missing mandatory permit, inaccurate declaration request, serious cargo incident or unauthorised contract commitment. Escalate commercial changes when they exceed approved price, credit, time or risk thresholds.

## 8. Practical assignment
Build an authority matrix for a 10-person import/export team. Define who can:
- Approve suppliers.
- Sign contracts and purchase orders.
- Approve discounts and credit.
- Select freight providers.
- Approve bank details.
- Authorise customs declarations.
- Approve insurance claims.
- Accept damaged/short cargo.
Add thresholds, substitute approvers and audit evidence.

## 9. Knowledge check
1. Does responsibility automatically mean authority? **No.**
2. Why have one accountable owner in RACI? **To avoid ambiguity over outcome ownership.**
3. What is segregation of duties? **Separating incompatible tasks to reduce error/fraud.**
4. Can a manager override a legal prohibition? **No.**

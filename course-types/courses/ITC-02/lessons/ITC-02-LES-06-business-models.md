# ITC-02-LES-06 — B2B, B2C, D2C and Marketplace Models

**Level:** Intermediate | **Study time:** 75–90 minutes | **Languages:** English, Hindi, Thai

## 1. Learning outcomes
Distinguish major sales-channel models; identify the seller of record, buyer type, payment flow, fulfilment responsibilities and consumer obligations.

## 2. Business models
- **B2B — Business to Business:** A business sells to another business, often with negotiated pricing, volume, credit and repeat orders.
- **B2C — Business to Consumer:** A business sells to an individual consumer, often with consumer-protection, product-information, returns and tax considerations.
- **D2C — Direct to Consumer:** A brand or manufacturer sells directly to end consumers without a traditional wholesale intermediary. D2C is a channel strategy and can still use third-party fulfilment.
- **Marketplace:** A platform connects sellers and buyers. It may facilitate listings, payments, fulfilment or customer service; its legal role differs by platform and country.
- **B2B2C:** A business reaches consumers through another business or platform.
- **Wholesale:** Goods sold in larger quantities, usually for resale or business use.
- **Retail:** Goods sold to end users, commonly in smaller quantities.

## 3. Compare the operating model
| Dimension | B2B | B2C | D2C | Marketplace |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Customer | Business | Consumer | Consumer | Depends on platform |
| Order size | Often larger | Often smaller | Often smaller | Varies |
| Pricing | Negotiated/contract | Listed/promotional | Brand-controlled in many cases | Seller/platform rules |
| Sales cycle | Often longer | Often shorter | Digital/brand-led | Platform-led discovery |
| Service | Account management | Consumer support | Brand-owned relationship | Shared/allocated by terms |
| Compliance | Product/import/commercial | Also consumer rules | Also direct-sale rules | Platform and seller obligations |

## 4. Cross-border channel questions
- Who is seller of record?
- Who is importer of record?
- Who collects payment and issues the invoice?
- Who sets price and handles refunds?
- Who is responsible for product labelling, warranties and recalls?
- Who handles customer data and privacy?
- Who fulfils the order and bears return freight?
- Are duties/taxes collected at checkout or on import?
- Is the transaction business or consumer for relevant legal/tax purposes?

Never assume that a marketplace collects all taxes or becomes importer of record. Read platform terms and destination-country law.

## 5. Worked examples
**B2B:** A Thai wholesaler imports 1,000 components and resells to factories.  
**B2C:** A retailer imports 20 coffee machines and sells each to a household.  
**D2C:** A brand sells its products through its own website and ships directly to consumers.  
**Marketplace:** Independent merchants list products on a platform; the platform processes payment but each merchant may remain the seller.

## 6. Unit economics
For channel analysis, calculate:
Revenue per order − product cost − freight/fulfilment − payment/platform fees − returns allowance − duties/taxes borne by seller − customer acquisition cost = contribution before overhead (define treatment consistently).

Do not confuse revenue with profit. Include refunds, chargebacks, warranty and customer-service costs.

## 7. Practical assignment
For one product, design B2B, B2C and D2C channel maps. Identify seller, buyer, invoice issuer, payment recipient, importer, fulfilment provider, return handler and main regulatory questions. Build a comparison of cost, margin, lead time, customer experience and risk.

## 8. Knowledge check
1. Is D2C simply another name for B2B? **No; it means direct selling to consumers.**
2. Does every marketplace become seller of record? **No.**
3. Name three extra concerns in B2C cross-border sales. **Consumer rights, product information/labeling, returns, privacy, tax/duty disclosure.**
4. Why calculate contribution per order? **To understand economics after variable costs, not just sales revenue.**

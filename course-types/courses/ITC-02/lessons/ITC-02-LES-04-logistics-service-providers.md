# ITC-02-LES-04 — Logistics and Trade Service Providers

**Level:** Intermediate | **Study time:** 90 minutes | **Languages:** English, Hindi, Thai

## 1. Learning outcomes
Distinguish forwarder, carrier, NVOCC, terminal, warehouse, customs broker, surveyor and inspection body; evaluate service scope and hand-off evidence.

## 2. Provider roles
- **Freight forwarder:** Organises movement and related services. May act as agent or contract as principal.
- **Carrier:** Undertakes carriage and operates or contracts transport capacity.
- **NVOCC:** In ocean freight, a non-vessel-operating common carrier may issue its own transport document and contract carriage without operating the vessel; legal status varies by jurisdiction.
- **Terminal operator:** Handles cargo at a port, airport or inland terminal.
- **Warehouse operator:** Receives, stores, handles and releases inventory under agreed controls.
- **Customs broker:** Provides customs representation or declaration services where authorised.
- **Surveyor:** Inspects or assesses cargo condition, quantity or damage within agreed scope.
- **Inspection/testing body:** Performs specified checks, sampling or tests; accreditation and scope must be verified.
- **Trucking/rail operator:** Performs inland or rail movement under a transport contract.

## 3. Service scope checklist
Before appointment, confirm:
1. Legal entity, licence/authorisation and service locations.
2. Whether provider acts as agent or principal.
3. Exact scope: booking, pickup, export, customs, main carriage, insurance, delivery.
4. Included and excluded charges.
5. Liability limits, claims deadlines and insurance.
6. Data/security requirements and subcontracting.
7. Cut-off times, operating hours and escalation contacts.
8. Service-level targets and reporting frequency.

## 4. Quote comparison
Compare like-for-like:
- Origin pickup and handling.
- Export clearance and documentation.
- Main freight and surcharges.
- Transshipment and destination handling.
- Import clearance and duties/taxes (state exclusions).
- Delivery, waiting, storage and demurrage/detention assumptions.
- Transit time range, route and frequency.
- Validity, currency, payment terms and free-time conditions.

A low base freight rate may conceal destination charges or exclusions. Ask for a complete written breakdown.

## 5. Handover evidence
For each hand-off, record date/time, location, party, cargo count/condition, seal number where applicable, document reference and exceptions. Evidence may include gate-in/out records, warehouse receipts, interchange receipts, carrier receipts, transport documents and proof of delivery.

## 6. Worked example
A forwarder books ocean carriage with a carrier and arranges trucking through a subcontractor. The forwarder is the buyer's coordination contact, but the carrier performs the ocean leg and the truck operator performs inland carriage. The coordinator records separate references and responsible contacts for each leg.

## 7. Performance measures
- On-time pickup and delivery.
- Schedule reliability.
- Document error rate.
- Quote accuracy and cost variance.
- Exception response time.
- Cargo damage/claim frequency.
- Customs document readiness (not guaranteed clearance time).

Do not compare providers using one KPI alone; consider service scope and cargo suitability.

## 8. Practical exercise
Build a provider scorecard with mandatory gates (licence, safety, insurance, compliance) and weighted measures (cost, route, transit time, service, visibility, claims handling). A provider failing a mandatory gate cannot pass through a high weighted score.

## 9. Knowledge check
1. Is a forwarder always the ocean carrier? **No.**
2. What is the key NVOCC distinction? **It may contract as carrier and issue its own document without operating the vessel.**
3. Why compare all-in charges? **To avoid misleading base-rate comparisons.**
4. What should be recorded at cargo handover? **Time, location, parties, quantity/condition, seal and exceptions.**

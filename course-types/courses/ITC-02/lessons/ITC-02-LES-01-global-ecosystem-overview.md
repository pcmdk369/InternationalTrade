# ITC-02-LES-01 — Global Trade Ecosystem Overview

**Level:** Intermediate | **Study time:** 75–90 minutes | **Languages:** English, Hindi, Thai

## 1. Learning outcomes
Explain what the global trade ecosystem is; group participants by function; trace information, goods, money and regulatory control; distinguish a participant from an authority.

## 2. Definition
The **global trade ecosystem** is the network of commercial organisations, logistics providers, financial institutions, insurers, public authorities, standards bodies and infrastructure operators that enable or regulate cross-border exchange.

**हिंदी:** Global Trade Ecosystem अंतरराष्ट्रीय व्यापार में शामिल कंपनियों, सेवा प्रदाताओं, वित्तीय संस्थानों, सरकारी निकायों और परिवहन ढाँचे का पूरा नेटवर्क है।

## 3. Four flows in a transaction
1. **Goods flow:** factory → warehouse → port/airport → carrier → destination terminal → buyer.
2. **Information flow:** inquiry, specification, purchase order, booking, shipping instructions, status and exception messages.
3. **Money flow:** buyer payment, bank settlement, financing, insurance premium, freight and duty/tax payments.
4. **Control/evidence flow:** licences, declarations, inspection records, transport documents, proof of delivery and audit trail.

These flows interact but do not necessarily follow the same route or timing. Payment may occur before dispatch, against documents or after delivery depending on the contract and bank arrangement.

## 4. Ecosystem layers
| Layer | Typical participants | Core purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial | Buyer, seller, manufacturer, trader, distributor, agent | Agree what is sold and on what terms |
| Logistics | Forwarder, carrier, terminal, warehouse, inspection provider | Move, handle, store and inspect cargo |
| Financial | Banks, payment providers, finance providers | Settle payments and provide financing |
| Risk transfer | Cargo insurer, liability insurer | Cover specified risks under policy |
| Public control | Customs, licensing and standards authorities | Apply laws and regulatory controls |
| Support network | Chambers, trade associations, standards bodies | Information, networks, standards and support |

## 5. Map the transaction
Example: a Thai importer buys machine parts from a Korean manufacturer.
- Commercial parties agree specification, price, quantity and delivery.
- Manufacturer produces and packs goods.
- Seller/exporter coordinates export formalities.
- Forwarder arranges carriage; carrier physically transports cargo.
- Customs authorities apply origin and destination procedures.
- Authorised broker may support declarations.
- Bank processes payment under agreed terms.
- Insurer covers only risks specified by the policy.
- Importer receives goods and checks condition and quantity.

A party may perform multiple roles, but record each role separately.

## 6. Questions a coordinator must ask
- Who is contracting with whom?
- Who owns the goods at each point, according to the contract?
- Who physically holds the cargo?
- Who books and pays the carrier?
- Who is responsible for export and import formalities?
- Who is authorised to submit declarations?
- Which bank/payment arrangement applies?
- What insurance exists, who is insured and what exclusions apply?
- Which authority has jurisdiction over the product and route?

## 7. Common mistakes
- Treating every participant as a “supplier”.
- Assuming a forwarder owns or carries the cargo.
- Assuming a bank guarantees product quality.
- Assuming insurance covers every delay or loss.
- Treating a trade association as a regulator.
- Confusing a service provider's commercial advice with an official customs decision.

## 8. Practical exercise
Draw four parallel lanes—goods, information, money and regulatory evidence—for one import transaction. Identify 12 participants, show hand-offs and mark at least three points where delays or errors can occur.

## 9. Knowledge check
1. Name the four transaction flows. **Goods, information, money, control/evidence.**
2. Is a chamber of commerce normally a customs authority? **No.**
3. Does a cargo insurer cover every possible loss? **No; policy terms, exclusions and conditions govern.**
4. Why map roles separately? **One organisation may perform several roles, and responsibility depends on contract and law.**

# ITC-02-LES-03 — Distributors, Agents and Intermediaries

**Level:** Intermediate | **Study time:** 75–90 minutes | **Languages:** English, Hindi, Thai

## 1. Learning outcomes
Compare distributors, commercial agents, brokers, sourcing agents, trading companies and marketplace operators; identify who buys, who sells and who earns a fee or margin.

## 2. Definitions
- **Distributor:** Usually buys products and resells them in an agreed market, often holding inventory and taking resale risk.
- **Commercial agent:** Usually introduces or negotiates business on behalf of a principal for commission; may not take title to goods. Legal status varies by jurisdiction.
- **Broker:** Introduces or brings parties together for a transaction, generally for a fee or commission; authority depends on appointment.
- **Sourcing agent:** Helps identify, evaluate or coordinate suppliers; scope may include sampling, negotiation and inspection.
- **Trading company:** Buys and resells goods, or otherwise intermediates trade as principal depending on structure.
- **Reseller:** Purchases products for resale, often through a defined channel.
- **Marketplace operator:** Provides a digital platform connecting participants; may or may not be seller, merchant of record, payment handler or logistics provider.

## 3. Compare commercial structure
| Model | Takes title to goods? | Typical revenue | Main exposure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distributor | Often yes | Resale margin | Inventory, credit, market |
| Agent | Usually no | Commission | Authority, performance, legal agency |
| Broker | Usually no | Brokerage fee | Misrepresentation, scope disputes |
| Sourcing agent | Usually no | Fee/commission | Supplier quality, conflicts |
| Trading company | Often yes when principal | Trading margin | Price, inventory, counterparty |
| Marketplace | Depends on model | Commission/listing/payment/service fee | Platform, consumer, payment and data obligations |

These are common patterns, not universal legal definitions. Contract and local law control.

## 4. Principal vs agent
A **principal** contracts in its own name and bears contractual obligations. An **agent** acts with authority on behalf of a principal within an agreed scope. A coordinator must know:
- Who signs the sales contract?
- Who issues the invoice?
- Who receives payment?
- Who owns the goods and when?
- Can the intermediary bind the principal?
- Is the intermediary allowed to negotiate price or approve changes?
- How is the commission calculated and disclosed?

Never allow an intermediary to promise discounts, delivery, warranty or compliance approvals outside written authority.

## 5. Commission and conflict controls
A written intermediary agreement should address scope, territory, exclusivity, fee basis, payment trigger, expenses, confidentiality, anti-bribery obligations, conflicts of interest, records, termination and post-termination commission. Obtain legal review in relevant jurisdictions.

Disclose material commissions and avoid undisclosed double representation. Do not pay facilitation or improper payments.

## 6. Worked example
A buyer appoints a sourcing agent to identify factories. The buyer signs the purchase contract directly with the selected manufacturer and pays the manufacturer. The agent invoices a disclosed sourcing fee. The agent is not automatically the seller, exporter or owner of the goods.

Alternative: a trading company buys products from the factory and resells them to the buyer. It is the seller under its resale contract and may take inventory and credit risk.

## 7. Practical assignment
Compare three proposed routes: direct purchase from factory, purchase through distributor, and sourcing agent arrangement. For each, map contract parties, invoice issuer, payment recipient, title/ownership terms, commission/margin, warranty path, risk owner and required due diligence.

## 8. Knowledge check
1. Does an agent necessarily own the goods? **No.**
2. What determines whether an agent can bind a principal? **Written authority, applicable law and the agency arrangement.**
3. What must be clear about commission? **Amount/basis, trigger, recipient, disclosure and conflicts.**
4. Is a marketplace always the seller? **No; identify its actual contractual role.**

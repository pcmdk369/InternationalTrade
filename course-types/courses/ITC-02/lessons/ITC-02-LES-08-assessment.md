# ITC-02-LES-08 — Global Trade Ecosystem Assessment

**Level:** Intermediate | **Study time:** 2 hours

## 1. Assessment weights
- Knowledge quiz: 20%
- Ecosystem map: 20%
- Role and authority matrix: 25%
- Integrated case: 35%

Suggested pass: 70%. A critical failure involving fraud controls, false declarations, unauthorised commitments or legal authority must be remediated.

## 2. Knowledge quiz
1. Name the four flows in a trade transaction.
2. Distinguish buyer, seller, manufacturer and importer.
3. How does a distributor commonly differ from an agent?
4. Is a freight forwarder always the carrier?
5. What is an NVOCC in ocean transport?
6. Does a bank examining documents physically inspect the cargo?
7. Is a chamber of commerce automatically a regulator?
8. Explain B2B, B2C and D2C.
9. What is the difference between responsibility and authority?
10. Give two examples of segregation of duties.

### Answer key
1. Goods, information, money, control/evidence.
2. Buyer purchases; seller contracts to sell; manufacturer produces; importer is the party identified/responsible under applicable import rules.
3. Distributor commonly buys/resells; agent commonly acts for a principal for commission without taking title.
4. No.
5. A non-vessel-operating common carrier may contract ocean carriage and issue its own transport document without operating the vessel.
6. No.
7. No; chamber services differ by jurisdiction and do not replace regulators.
8. Business-to-business, business-to-consumer, direct-to-consumer.
9. Responsibility is task duty; authority is permission/power to decide or bind.
10. Separate supplier setup from payment approval; separate bank-change entry from independent verification.

## 3. Integrated case
A Thai retailer sources a branded appliance through a Singapore trading company. The actual factory is in China. The shipment departs from China, travels by sea, and is delivered to Thailand. The buyer pays in USD. The retailer appoints a freight forwarder and a customs broker. A marketplace is being considered for future consumer sales.

### Tasks
A. Draw the ecosystem map and show goods, information, money and evidence flows.  
B. Identify seller, manufacturer, brand owner, exporter, importer, forwarder, carrier, broker, bank and insurer. Mark unknown facts rather than guessing.  
C. Compare buying directly from the factory, through the trading company or via a distributor.  
D. Identify which public authorities may need to be consulted for product and import requirements.  
E. Build a RACI and delegated authority matrix for supplier approval, product approval, shipment booking, customs data, payment and delivery acceptance.  
F. Identify at least eight risks and controls.  
G. Explain how the business model changes if goods are sold B2C via a marketplace.

## 4. Model answer guide
- Seller is the entity named in the purchase contract; manufacturer and brand owner may be different.
- Ship-from China does not alone prove country of origin.
- Forwarder coordinates according to its contract; carrier performs carriage; broker acts only within local authorisation.
- Bank handles agreed financial services, not product inspection.
- Identify the Thai competent product regulator and customs requirements based on the exact product/model and current rules.
- Marketplace role must be read from seller terms: seller of record, payment collection, fulfilment, returns, data and tax roles may differ.
- Do not approve product compliance or customs classification from a supplier's unsupported statement.

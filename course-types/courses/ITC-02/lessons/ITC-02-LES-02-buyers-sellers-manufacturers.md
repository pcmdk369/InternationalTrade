# ITC-02-LES-02 — Buyers, Sellers and Manufacturers

**Level:** Intermediate | **Study time:** 75 minutes | **Languages:** English, Hindi, Thai

## 1. Learning outcomes
Distinguish buyer, importer, seller, exporter, manufacturer and brand owner; understand why legal identity and role verification matter; build a party master record.

## 2. Role definitions
- **Buyer:** Commercial party purchasing goods or services under an agreement.
- **Seller:** Party that undertakes to sell and supply under the sales contract.
- **Importer:** Party identified under applicable import rules as responsible for bringing goods into the customs territory; local legal definitions differ.
- **Exporter:** Party responsible or identified for export under the relevant customs/legal framework.
- **Manufacturer:** Entity that physically produces or assembles the product.
- **Brand owner:** Entity that owns or controls the brand/trademark rights.
- **Consignor/shipper:** Party shown as sending/handing over goods in a transport context; document definitions and legal effects vary.
- **Consignee:** Party named to receive cargo in a transport document; not automatically the owner or importer.

## 3. Why the names may differ
A brand may outsource manufacturing. A trading company may purchase from a factory and resell to a foreign buyer. A logistics company may be named as shipper under a particular document without being the seller. Therefore, do not infer the full transaction from a single document field.

## 4. Party due-diligence file
Capture:
- Registered legal name and registration number (where available).
- Registered and operating addresses.
- Country of incorporation and tax identifiers where lawful.
- Authorised signatories and role evidence.
- Official website and independently verified contact details.
- Bank account beneficiary name and independently verified bank evidence.
- Product scope, capacity, quality system and references.
- Licences/permits relevant to the business or product.
- Sanctions/restricted-party screening result where applicable and lawful.
- Review date, reviewer, source and unresolved exceptions.

Collect only necessary personal data, protect it, and follow applicable privacy and retention laws.

## 5. Supplier verification workflow
1. Obtain legal details directly from the counterparty.
2. Verify company existence through an official registry or competent source.
3. Verify the person signing has authority.
4. Validate factory address and production capability through proportionate evidence or audit.
5. Confirm product and quality requirements.
6. Independently verify bank details before first payment.
7. Screen for applicable restrictions using current approved tools.
8. Record approval, conditions, expiry/review date and owner.

## 6. Worked example
A Thai distributor buys branded kitchen equipment from a Singapore trading company. The trading company sources from a factory in China. The invoice seller may be the Singapore company; manufacturer is the Chinese factory; goods ship from China; importer is the Thai buyer. These facts can all coexist. Confirm origin separately—ship-from location does not automatically determine origin.

## 7. Red flags
- Legal name differs between contract, invoice and bank beneficiary without explanation.
- Supplier refuses to identify manufacturer where this is material to compliance.
- Email domain or bank account changes unexpectedly.
- Factory audit evidence is reused, expired or unverifiable.
- Unusually low price with pressure for immediate advance payment.
- Company address, registration or signatory details cannot be verified.

## 8. Practical assignment
Create a party master record for a hypothetical transaction. Include legal identity, role, country, verified sources, authority evidence, risk flags and approval decision. Separate confirmed facts from assumptions.

## 9. Knowledge check
1. Is manufacturer always the seller? **No.**
2. Is ship-from country always country of origin? **No.**
3. What is a safe bank verification channel? **A previously verified contact route independent of the change request.**
4. Why record source and review date? **To make due diligence traceable and refreshable.**
